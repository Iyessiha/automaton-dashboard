## 1. MCP Tool Poisoning in Agentic AI Frameworks: A Cross-Framework Measurement Study
*Relevance: 271%*

This paper addresses **MCP tool poisoning attacks** in agentic AI systems, where attacker-controlled content (malicious tool descriptions, schemas, responses) enters LLM context without validation, potentially hijacking agent behavior. The authors conduct the first cross-framework measurement study with real tool execution across 12 frameworks, introducing a 25-class root-cause taxonomy and open-source tool (mcpvenom) to systematically evaluate 9,720+ attack scenarios.

**Why it matters:** ML practitioners building agentic systems with MCP integrations face serious security risks, as this study demonstrates that poisoning vulnerabilities persist across major frameworks under realistic conditions—highlighting the urgent need for input validation and defensive measures when connecting LLMs to external tools.

→ http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03555v1

## 2. A Scaling Theory of Multi-Epoch Pretraining: Late-Time Overfitting and Ensembling
*Relevance: 235%*

This paper addresses the problem of data-limited language model pretraining, where models must train for multiple epochs on fixed datasets, leading to late-time overfitting and performance deterioration. The authors develop a scaling theory for multi-epoch training and demonstrate that logit averaging (ensembling) reduces late-training degradation, delays optimal stopping points, and outperforms larger single models at equivalent parameter-token budgets.

This matters to ML practitioners because as high-quality pretraining data becomes exhausted, understanding how to effectively reuse data through multi-epoch training and ensembling strategies will be essential for continued model improvements without requiring new data sources.

→ http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03590v1

## 3. Tracing the Scientific Discovery Capacity of LLMs to Their Training Data
*Relevance: 234%*

This paper addresses the problem of understanding what enables LLMs to make scientific discoveries by tracing this capacity back to training data composition. The authors develop a theoretical framework identifying "monofacts" (facts seen only once during training) as key determinants of discovery capacity, validating this through experiments in materials science and genomics where manipulating monofact rates directly affected discovery outcomes.

**Why it matters:** ML practitioners can use these insights to deliberately curate training data for discovery-oriented models, potentially improving scientific applications by optimizing the distribution of rare factual content rather than just scaling data volume.

→ http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03617v1

## 4. Pretraining Data Scale Reverses the Muon-AdamW Fine-Tuning Gap
*Relevance: 226%*

This paper reveals that while the Muon optimizer outperforms AdamW during pretraining, this advantage reverses after fine-tuning when pretraining data budgets are sufficiently large, with AdamW achieving better downstream performance—and this crossover occurs at smaller data-to-parameter ratios as model size increases. For ML practitioners, this finding suggests that optimizer choice should consider the entire training pipeline rather than just pretraining metrics, and that AdamW may remain preferable for large-scale models intended for fine-tuning despite Muon's pretraining efficiency gains.

→ http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03633v1

## 5. Online Label-Free Gradient Matching for Continual Language Model Training
*Relevance: 219%*

**Summary:**

OLGA solves the problem of efficiently updating language models on continuously growing data streams without requiring ground-truth labels, addressing limitations of existing gradient matching methods that need labels, work only on fixed snapshots, and scale poorly. The method enables online, label-free gradient matching for continual learning, which matters to ML practitioners because it allows practical, efficient model updates for customer-facing products where data shifts over time and full retraining is infeasible.

→ http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03496v1
