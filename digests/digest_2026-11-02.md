## 1. SecPO: Principled Adversarial Training for Prompt Injection Security
*Relevance: 250%*

SecPO addresses prompt injection attacks on AI agents by replacing DPO's insecure reference model with an approximated injection-invariant oracle, significantly reducing attack success rates that approached 100% against previous defenses like Meta-SecAlign. This principled adversarial training approach matters to ML practitioners because prompt injection remains a critical security threat for LLM-based agents processing untrusted data, and SecPO offers a more robust defense methodology.

→ http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41998v1

## 2. Subquadratic Attention with a Hierarchical Tree-Structured Indexer
*Relevance: 247%*

This paper addresses the quadratic computational scaling of attention in LLMs by introducing Zyphra Hie, a hierarchical tree-structured indexer that enables asymptotically subquadratic sparse attention while maintaining expressiveness. For ML practitioners, this matters because it offers a path to efficiently scaling long-context capabilities without the typical trade-offs of reduced expressiveness (like linear attention) or only constant-factor speedups (like existing sparse methods).

→ http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.42112v1

## 3. Latent Sparse Attention: Towards Subquadratic Ultra-Long Context Attention
*Relevance: 244%*

This paper addresses the prohibitive quadratic cost of full attention for ultra-long contexts (1M+ tokens) in LLMs, which limits agentic workflows requiring extended reasoning. The authors propose Latent Sparse Attention (LSA), a sparse attention method that efficiently indexes and selects relevant keys while maintaining accuracy at ultra-long context scales—enabling ML practitioners to build models capable of handling long-horizon tasks like open-ended scientific work without the computational burden of full attention.

→ http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.42147v1

## 4. CRoS: Causality Reward on Scrambled CoTs Improves Accuracy and Reasoning Faithfulness
*Relevance: 226%*

**Summary:**

This paper addresses the problem that standard RLVR training rewards correct answers without ensuring the chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning actually contributes to those answers, potentially reinforcing unfaithful reasoning. The authors propose CRoS (Causality Reward on Scrambled CoTs), which uses scrambled reasoning steps as perturbations to measure and reward causal connections between CoT and answers, improving both accuracy and reasoning faithfulness—important for ML practitioners seeking models with reliable, interpretable reasoning rather than just correct outputs.

→ http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.42054v1

## 5. Prune Gradually, Adapt Sparsely: Efficient Pruning of Large Language Models at Extreme Sparsity
*Relevance: 222%*

This paper addresses the challenge of maintaining LLM quality when pruning to extreme sparsity levels, where one-shot methods degrade sharply and existing optimization approaches require prohibitively expensive model-sized optimizer states. The authors propose a method combining gradual pruning with sparse adaptation that achieves state-of-the-art quality at high sparsity while significantly reducing computational costs, making extreme sparsity pruning more practical for ML practitioners working with resource-constrained deployment scenarios.

→ http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41968v1
