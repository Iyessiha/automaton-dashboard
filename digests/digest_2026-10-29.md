## 1. Follow the Gradient: Tracing Safety Degradation Back to Training Data
*Relevance: 248%*

**Summary:**

This paper addresses the problem of hidden safety degradation in fine-tuned LLMs caused by benign-looking training data that evades standard content filters. The authors develop SimpleLoRIF, a scalable gradient embedding method that identifies problematic training examples by detecting which ones have gradients aligned with unsafe model behavior, enabling analysis of million-scale datasets through low-rank attribution and restricted backpropagation. This matters to ML practitioners because it provides a practical tool for auditing fine-tuning datasets to prevent unintentional safety erosion before deployment.

→ http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37481v1

## 2. Collective Dynamics of Self-Reinforcement: How Cross-Task Coupling Drives Policy Collapse in Unsupervised RLVR
*Relevance: 238%*

**Summary:** This paper identifies and explains "policy collapse" in unsupervised RLVR (where language models are rewarded for agreeing with their own majority vote), showing that training on many problems creates a collective instability where cross-task parameter sharing amplifies perturbations until the model outputs identical answers for all inputs. For ML practitioners, this reveals a fundamental scaling limitation of self-consistency-based reward signals and provides theoretical grounding for why unsupervised verification methods may fail catastrophically as training data grows.

→ http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37553v1

## 3. Scaling Laws for Tool-Use Agents via Controllable Environment Synthesis
*Relevance: 232%*

**Summary:**

This paper addresses the challenge of training LLM tool-use agents for complex multi-step tasks by introducing SynthAPI, a pipeline that systematically controls the diversity of synthetically generated training environments across tools, tasks, and databases. The derived scaling laws from extensive experiments (458,752 tasks, 849 models) provide ML practitioners with predictive guidance on how data composition affects agent performance, enabling more efficient training data curation for tool-use applications.

→ http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37648v1

## 4. Heads of Security: Learned Attention Gates Improve Prompt Injection Robustness
*Relevance: 228%*

**Summary:** Heads of Security (HoS) addresses prompt injection attacks in LLMs by learning per-head attention gates that enforce instruction-data separation, allowing models to process untrusted content without executing malicious instructions embedded within it. This matters to ML practitioners because it offers an architectural defense mechanism that can improve LLM security against adversarial inputs while preserving the model's ability to reason over external data.

→ http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37657v1

## 5. Orthogonal and Parametrized Multi-Attribute Steering of LLMs
*Relevance: 226%*

**Summary:** This paper addresses the problem of inconsistent steering strength and lack of interpretability when modifying LLM behavior through activation steering, solving it by identifying optimal steerable intervals for each vector and introducing "parameterized steering" that sets activations to specific trait levels rather than pushing them in a direction. For ML practitioners, this matters because it enables more reliable, interpretable control over LLM outputs at inference time without retraining, making behavioral modifications more predictable and easier to calibrate across different attributes.

→ http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37517v1
