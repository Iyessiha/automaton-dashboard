## 1. Training Foundation Models with a Single Algorithm
*Relevance: 282%*

This paper addresses the complexity of multi-stage foundation model training (pretraining, SFT, RL) by unifying all stages under a single likelihood-based algorithm, reframing RL objectives as maximum-likelihood fitting to model-generated target distributions. For ML practitioners, this simplifies the training pipeline by reducing algorithmic complexity to data selection decisions—what data to use, in what proportions, and when—potentially streamlining foundation model development and making the training lifecycle more interpretable and manageable.

→ http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40564v1

## 2. On the Fundamental Limits of Prefill-Based Decoding Length Prediction
*Relevance: 237%*

This paper addresses the problem of predicting LLM output length from prefill states for inference scheduling, revealing that current benchmark accuracy is misleading because it conflates predictor quality with task difficulty and ignores the inherent randomness in autoregressive decoding. The authors derive fundamental theoretical bounds that limit what any prefill-based prediction method can achieve, which matters to ML practitioners because it establishes realistic expectations for scheduling systems and explains why predictor performance varies dramatically across models, datasets, and sampling configurations.

→ http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40541v1

## 3. Collaborative AI agents for autonomous experimental research with solid-state quantum spins
*Relevance: 236%*

This paper presents an autonomous AI research laboratory using collaborative large language model agents that assess each other's work, addressing the problem of AI systems drawing unsupported conclusions by enabling agents to stop, redirect, or reject investigations through mutual oversight and theoretical checks.

For ML practitioners, this matters because it demonstrates a practical framework for building more reliable autonomous AI systems through multi-agent collaboration and self-correction mechanisms, particularly relevant for applications requiring high scientific rigor.

→ http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40589v1

## 4. Preserving Utility in Continual LLM Unlearning via Orthogonal Projections
*Relevance: 236%*

This paper addresses the problem of catastrophic utility decay in large language models when performing sequential unlearning requests, where each removal operation interferes with previous deletions and degrades model performance. The authors propose using orthogonal projections to constrain unlearning updates, preventing interference between successive removals—a method that matters to ML practitioners who need to continuously remove sensitive, copyrighted, or private data from deployed models without repeated full retraining.

→ http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40483v1

## 5. How I Aligned Your Model: Scaling Up Multilingual Safety Training with Templates
*Relevance: 232%*

This paper addresses the problem of limited multilingual safety alignment in LLMs, particularly for low-resource languages lacking culturally-localized harmful topic coverage. The authors propose a scalable template-based augmentation pipeline that extracts safe templates from harmful prompts, translates them, and fills slots with culturally authentic entities to generate massive multilingual refusal and benign training corpora.

This matters to ML practitioners because it provides a cost-effective method to extend safety training beyond English-centric datasets, enabling more robust and culturally appropriate alignment across diverse languages without requiring extensive manual annotation.

→ http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40516v1
