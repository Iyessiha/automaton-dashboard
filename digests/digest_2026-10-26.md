## 1. On-Policy Self-Training: Data-Free Self-Improvement in Large Language Models
*Relevance: 247%*

This paper addresses the problem of improving large language models without requiring external teachers, labeled datasets, or human-curated problems by introducing a self-training framework where models generate their own question-answer pairs and reasoning demonstrations guided only by high-level topic descriptions. For ML practitioners, this matters because it enables data-free post-training improvement of foundation models, potentially reducing dependence on expensive human annotation and external supervision while allowing models to autonomously create and learn from their own curriculum.

→ http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32770v1

## 2. NgramRL: Accelerating RL Post-Training for Structured Generation via Rollout-Aware N-gram Speculative Decoding
*Relevance: 232%*

**Summary:**

NgramRL solves the problem of slow reinforcement learning post-training for LLMs by introducing a rollout-aware n-gram speculative decoding method that exploits repetitive patterns across training epochs, using recent rollouts from the same prompt as draft sources instead of requiring separate draft models. This matters to ML practitioners because it accelerates RL training (where autoregressive rollout generation consumes over 70% of training time) without the GPU memory overhead or policy misalignment issues of model-based drafters.

→ http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32881v1

## 3. Interpretable sparse fine-tuning with TopKLoRA enables faithful circuit discovery
*Relevance: 229%*

**Summary:**

TopKLoRA solves the problem of localizing and removing backdoors introduced during fine-tuning on poisoned data by creating an interpretable-by-design LoRA variant where only the k largest adapter latents are active per token. This matters to ML practitioners because it enables faithful circuit discovery and backdoor detection while maintaining parameter efficiency, offering advantages over post-hoc interpretability methods like SAEs by being inherently interpretable during training.

→ http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32866v1

## 4. Where Rounding Happens Matters: Rounding-Location Alignment Stabilizes LLM Reinforcement Learning
*Relevance: 226%*

**Summary:**

This paper addresses the problem of training instability in LLM reinforcement learning caused by numerical differences between separate inference and training systems, even when using identical BF16 weights. The authors propose selectively aligning rounding locations in specific network regions (RoPE, SwiGLU) rather than requiring full stack alignment or precision changes, offering ML practitioners a targeted, efficient fix for maintaining on-policy updates without overhauling their entire infrastructure.

→ http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32951v1

## 5. Direction-Aware Token Credit Assignment with Reward-Model Gradients
*Relevance: 225%*

**Summary:**

DATC solves the problem of imprecise token-level credit assignment in GRPO, where shared response-level advantages fail to capture individual token contributions to reward. The method uses frozen reward-model gradients to compute direction-aware credit scores that measure each token's local reward change along the policy update direction, enabling more targeted reinforcement learning updates that better align token-level learning signals with actual reward improvements.

→ http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32772v1
