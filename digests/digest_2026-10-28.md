## 1. Follow the Gradient: Tracing Safety Degradation Back to Training Data
*Relevance: 244%*

**Summary:**

This paper addresses the problem of hidden safety degradation in fine-tuned LLMs caused by benign-looking training data that evades standard content filters. The authors develop SimpleLoRIF, a scalable gradient embedding method that identifies problematic training examples by detecting which ones have gradients aligned with unsafe model behavior, achieving efficiency through low-rank attribution and restricting backpropagation to final transformer blocks. This matters to ML practitioners because it provides a practical tool for auditing million-scale fine-tuning datasets to prevent unintentional safety erosion without relying solely on content-based filtering.

→ http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37481v1

## 2. Collective Dynamics of Self-Reinforcement: How Cross-Task Coupling Drives Policy Collapse in Unsupervised RLVR
*Relevance: 238%*

This paper explains why unsupervised RLVR (where models are rewarded for agreeing with their own majority vote) initially improves reasoning but eventually collapses into giving identical answers to all problems, with collapse occurring only when training on many problems. The authors model this as a collective instability where shared parameters across problems amplify perturbations, providing ML practitioners with theoretical insight into a critical failure mode of self-supervised reward schemes for language model reasoning.

→ http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37553v1

## 3. Scaling Laws for Tool-Use Agents via Controllable Environment Synthesis
*Relevance: 232%*

**Summary:**

This paper addresses the challenge of training effective LLM tool-use agents by proposing SynthAPI, a pipeline that systematically controls the diversity of synthetically generated training environments across tools, tasks, and databases. Through extensive experiments with 849 models across 458,752 tasks, the authors derive scaling laws showing how data composition affects agent performance, providing ML practitioners with principled guidance for generating synthetic training data to build better multi-step tool-use agents.

→ http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37648v1

## 4. Orthogonal and Parametrized Multi-Attribute Steering of LLMs
*Relevance: 229%*

**Summary:** This paper solves the problem of inconsistent steering strength and lack of interpretability when modifying LLM behavior through activation steering, by identifying optimal steerable intervals for each vector and introducing "parameterized steering" that sets activations to specific trait strengths rather than pushing them in a direction. For ML practitioners, this enables more reliable and interpretable control over LLM outputs at inference time without retraining, making behavioral modifications more predictable and easier to calibrate across different attributes.

→ http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37517v1

## 5. Heads of Security: Learned Attention Gates Improve Prompt Injection Robustness
*Relevance: 227%*

**Summary:** Heads of Security (HoS) addresses prompt injection attacks in LLMs by learning per-head attention gates that enforce separation between trusted instructions and untrusted data, allowing models to process untrusted content without executing injected commands. This matters to ML practitioners because it offers an architectural defense mechanism that outperforms existing approaches while preserving model utility—critical for deploying LLMs safely in applications handling external inputs.

→ http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37657v1
