## 1. Tev: Scaling Long-Horizon Agentic Environments with Dense Progress Rewards
*Relevance: 252%*

**Summary:** This paper addresses the challenge of training long-horizon autonomous agents, which struggle due to sparse rewards and lack of training data for complex, extended tasks. The authors introduce Tev-Gym, a training dataset with dense, progressive rewards that provide continuous feedback signals, enabling more effective learning for agents working on economically valuable tasks over extended periods—critical for ML practitioners developing autonomous systems and recursive self-improvement capabilities.

→ http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18868v1

## 2. Enabling Token-wise Importance Sampling in Off-Policy RLVR with Proximal KL Regularization
*Relevance: 251%*

This paper solves the problem of biased policy gradients when using token-wise importance sampling with off-policy data in reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR), which commonly occurs due to asynchronous generation and response reuse. The authors prove that adding full future KL credit to the proximal optimizer eliminates this bias, enabling stable off-policy learning without requiring full-response importance weights or fitted critics.

**Why it matters:** ML practitioners training LLMs with RLVR can now safely reuse off-policy rollouts and handle training-generation mismatches, improving sample efficiency and enabling more practical asynchronous training pipelines.

→ http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18738v1

## 3. PreciseCoder: Faithful and Generalizable Debugging Beyond Unit-Test Rewards
*Relevance: 234%*

**Summary:**

PreciseCoder addresses the problem that training code models with unit-test-only rewards leads to over-editing and hallucinated bugs in already-correct code during debugging. The method augments unit-test rewards with a signal for semantically verified, localized edits, resulting in smaller, more precise changes and higher pass rates across different model sizes.

→ http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18755v1

## 4. SEAR: Self-Evolving Agentic Robotics
*Relevance: 230%*

SEAR introduces a framework and benchmark for evaluating whether language-model agents can genuinely learn and improve robot control over time, distinguishing true skill acquisition from mere repeated attempts, while testing generalization across new tasks, simulators, and real robots. This matters to ML practitioners because it provides rigorous methodology for assessing agentic systems that autonomously write, test, and retain robot control code—addressing a critical gap in understanding whether frontier LLM-based robotics agents achieve meaningful self-improvement or simply benefit from increased sampling.

→ http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18792v1

## 5. SolBehave: Evaluating the Behavioural Correctness of LLM-Generated Smart Contracts
*Relevance: 228%*

This paper addresses the problem that LLM-generated Solidity smart contracts are evaluated primarily on compilation success rather than behavioral correctness—critical given that deployed contracts are immutable and permanently exploitable. The authors introduce SolBehave, a benchmark using differential fuzzing, historical-transaction replay, and synthetic-transaction replay across 250 real Ethereum contracts to systematically evaluate whether generated code behaves correctly, which matters to ML practitioners building code-generation models for high-stakes, irreversible deployment scenarios.

→ http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18745v1
