## 1. Training Foundation Models with a Single Algorithm
*Relevance: 280%*

This paper addresses the complexity of multi-stage foundation model training (pretraining, SFT, RL) by unifying all stages under a single likelihood-based algorithm, reframing RL objectives as maximum-likelihood fitting to model-generated target distributions. For ML practitioners, this simplification transforms the training pipeline from managing multiple algorithms into a single data-centric problem of selecting what data to use, in what proportions, and when—potentially streamlining infrastructure, reducing engineering complexity, and enabling more principled curriculum design across the entire training lifecycle.

→ http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40564v1

## 2. On the Fundamental Limits of Prefill-Based Decoding Length Prediction
*Relevance: 239%*

This paper addresses the problem of predicting LLM output length from prefill-phase hidden states for inference scheduling, revealing that current benchmark accuracy is misleading because it conflates predictor quality with task difficulty and ignores the inherent stochasticity of autoregressive decoding. The authors derive fundamental theoretical bounds that all prefill-based prediction methods must obey by treating decoding as a stopped stochastic process, which matters to ML practitioners because it explains why predictors fail when models, datasets, or sampling configurations change, and establishes realistic performance limits for scheduling systems.

→ http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40541v1

## 3. How I Aligned Your Model: Scaling Up Multilingual Safety Training with Templates
*Relevance: 235%*

This paper addresses the problem of limited multilingual safety alignment in LLMs, particularly for low-resource languages, by proposing a scalable template-based augmentation pipeline that extracts safe templates from harmful prompts, translates them, and fills slots with culturally authentic entities to generate massive multilingual refusal and benign training corpora. For ML practitioners, this matters because it offers a practical, scalable method to extend safety training beyond English-centric datasets while incorporating region-specific harmful topics and cultural contexts.

→ http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40516v1

## 4. Collaborative AI agents for autonomous experimental research with solid-state quantum spins
*Relevance: 234%*

This paper presents an autonomous research laboratory using collaborative large language model agents that can self-assess, challenge, and redirect their own scientific investigations with solid-state quantum spin experiments—addressing the critical problem of AI systems drawing unsupported conclusions by implementing built-in verification and rejection mechanisms.

For ML practitioners, this demonstrates a practical framework for deploying LLM agents in high-stakes scientific domains where reliability matters, showing how multi-agent collaboration with explicit checkpoints can mitigate hallucination and overconfidence issues inherent in single-agent systems.

→ http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40589v1

## 5. Preserving Utility in Continual LLM Unlearning via Orthogonal Projections
*Relevance: 231%*

This paper addresses the problem of **continual unlearning in LLMs**, where sequential data removal requests cause catastrophic interference—degrading both forgetting effectiveness and model utility over time. The authors propose using **orthogonal projections** to constrain each unlearning update to directions that don't interfere with previous removals, which matters to ML practitioners deploying LLMs under evolving privacy/copyright compliance requirements who need efficient, repeated unlearning without full retraining.

→ http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40483v1
