## 1. Beyond Causal Masks: Auditing and Localizing Hidden Future-Token Leakage in Adaptive Sequence Models
*Relevance: 230%*

This paper addresses the problem of hidden future-token leakage in autoregressive models, where architectural components (like adaptive routing mechanisms) can inadvertently expose the model to future tokens despite correct causal masking, leading to misleadingly good validation metrics but poor actual generation quality. The authors develop a prefix-invariance audit method to detect and localize such leakage, demonstrating its importance through a case study where a model showed dramatically lower validation loss but failed during free-running generation.

→ http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31522v1

## 2. Training Small Language Models for Transferable Tool Selection
*Relevance: 227%*

This paper addresses the challenge of selecting relevant tools from large tool libraries for LLM agents, where context limits prevent including all tool descriptions and simple retrievers lack task understanding. The authors train a small open-source language model using reinforcement learning to decompose complex instructions and select appropriate tools, achieving performance comparable to proprietary models like Claude or ChatGPT—offering ML practitioners a cost-effective, deployable alternative for tool-augmented AI systems.

→ http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31483v1

## 3. EpiSEM-Bench: Evaluating Safety and Evidence Recovery in LLM Agents under Missing Evidence
*Relevance: 221%*

This paper addresses the problem of LLM agents executing unsafe actions when tool responses omit safety-critical facts, even when all returned information is technically true—a gap that existing safety benchmarks don't evaluate. The authors introduce EpiSEM-Bench, an executable benchmark with 300 matched task pairs across six domains that systematically suppresses critical facts to measure unsafe execution rates and agents' ability to recover missing evidence, which matters to ML practitioners building reliable tool-using agents that must recognize when they lack sufficient information to act safely.

→ http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31650v1

## 4. Causal Chain of Thought: Mitigating Attention Leakage via Context Isolation
*Relevance: 220%*

**Summary:**

CausalCoT addresses the problem that chain-of-thought reasoning steps may not actually cause model outputs, as dense attention allows models to bypass reasoning via direct answer lookup while generating post-hoc justifications. The method enforces causal transparency by decomposing problems into directed acyclic graphs of atomic sub-questions with isolated context evaluation, which matters to ML practitioners because it provides more faithful, interpretable reasoning chains where intermediate steps genuinely influence final answers rather than serving as superficial explanations.

→ http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31639v1

## 5. Simulated Control Evals
*Relevance: 217%*

**Summary:**

This paper addresses the problem that traditional control evaluations for AI safety may not generalize well to real deployment environments due to unrepresented environmental features. The authors introduce simulated control evaluations (SCEs), which use language models to simulate tool responses instead of actual environments, enabling broader testing of safeguards against intentionally misaligned AI behavior.

**Why it matters:** ML practitioners developing safety-critical AI systems can use SCEs to more flexibly and comprehensively test whether their safeguards work across diverse deployment scenarios without building costly purpose-built evaluation environments.

→ http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31593v1
