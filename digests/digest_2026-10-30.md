## 1. Training Foundation Models with a Single Algorithm
*Relevance: 284%*

This paper addresses the complexity of multi-stage foundation model training (pretraining, SFT, RL) by unifying all stages under a single likelihood-based algorithm, reframing RL objectives as maximum-likelihood fitting to model-generated target distributions. For ML practitioners, this simplifies the training pipeline into purely data-centric decisions—choosing what data to use, in what proportions, and when—eliminating the need to switch between different learning algorithms across training stages.

→ http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40564v1

## 2. On the Fundamental Limits of Prefill-Based Decoding Length Prediction
*Relevance: 238%*

This paper addresses the problem of predicting LLM output length from prefill states for inference scheduling, revealing that current benchmark accuracy is misleading because it conflates predictor quality with task difficulty—accuracy collapses when models, datasets, or sampling configurations change. The authors derive fundamental theoretical bounds on prediction accuracy by modeling autoregressive decoding as a stochastic process, showing inherent limits that all prefill-based methods must obey due to decoding randomness.

→ http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40541v1

## 3. Collaborative AI agents for autonomous experimental research with solid-state quantum spins
*Relevance: 235%*

This paper presents an autonomous AI research laboratory using collaborative large language model agents that check each other's work to conduct solid-state quantum spin experiments, addressing the problem of AI systems drawing unsupported conclusions by implementing peer assessment and the ability to stop or redirect investigations. For ML practitioners, this demonstrates a practical framework for building more reliable autonomous AI systems through multi-agent collaboration and self-correction mechanisms, particularly relevant for applying LLMs to scientific experimentation and reducing hallucination-related errors.

→ http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40589v1

## 4. Preserving Utility in Continual LLM Unlearning via Orthogonal Projections
*Relevance: 233%*

This paper addresses the problem of **catastrophic utility decay in continual LLM unlearning**, where sequential data removal requests cause models to progressively lose both forgetting effectiveness and general performance due to interference between successive updates. The authors propose using **orthogonal projections** to constrain each unlearning update to directions that don't interfere with previous removals, preserving model utility across multiple sequential unlearning operations—critical for ML practitioners deploying LLMs that must continuously comply with privacy regulations and data deletion requests without costly retraining.

→ http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40483v1

## 5. How I Aligned Your Model: Scaling Up Multilingual Safety Training with Templates
*Relevance: 228%*

This paper addresses the problem of limited multilingual safety alignment in LLMs, particularly for low-resource languages lacking culturally-localized harmful topic coverage. The authors propose a scalable template-based augmentation pipeline that extracts safe templates from harmful prompts, translates them, and fills slots with culturally authentic entities to generate massive multilingual refusal and benign training corpora.

This matters to ML practitioners because it provides a cost-effective method to extend safety alignment beyond English-centric datasets, enabling more robust and culturally-aware content moderation across diverse languages without requiring extensive manual annotation for each language.

→ http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40516v1
