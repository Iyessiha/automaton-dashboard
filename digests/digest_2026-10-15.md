## 1. Your Transformer Is Secretly Elastic
*Relevance: 239%*

**Summary:**

This paper solves the problem of rigid transformer model sizing by introducing a retraining-free method that converts pre-trained transformers into "elastic" models capable of adapting dynamically across varying computational budgets. This matters to ML practitioners because it eliminates the need to maintain multiple model sizes, enabling a single model to efficiently handle heterogeneous hardware and fluctuating workloads without retraining.

→ http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20588v1

## 2. Lost in Translation: Why Lower Bias Scores Don't Mean Fairer Models
*Relevance: 237%*

**Summary:** This paper reveals that lower bias scores observed in non-English languages compared to English in multilingual models are largely measurement noise rather than genuine fairness improvements, exposing a fundamental flaw in cross-lingual bias auditing methodology. For ML practitioners, this matters because current multilingual bias benchmarks may provide misleading evidence of model fairness, requiring more robust evaluation approaches before deploying models across languages.

→ http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20479v1

## 3. Latent Compaction with Test-Time Training
*Relevance: 236%*

**Summary:** Latent Compaction addresses the problem of excessive memory and computational costs from large KV caches in long-context language models by learning compact, document-specific caches through test-time training, using initialization strategies based on reconstruction importance, teacher attention, and key-space diversity. This matters to ML practitioners because it enables efficient inference on long documents while preserving information needed for downstream tasks, offering a principled approach to cache compression that adapts to specific inputs rather than relying on static pruning methods.

→ http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20582v1

## 4. Beyond LLM Judge: Mechanical Verification for Agentic Autoformalization
*Relevance: 236%*

This paper addresses the problem of unreliable LLM-based verification in mathematical autoformalization by combining LLM semantic review with mechanical numerical and vacuity checks to catch errors that LLM judges miss. For ML practitioners, this matters because it provides more rigorous, checkable verification for agentic systems that automatically translate mathematical content into formal proofs, improving trustworthiness of automated formalization pipelines.

→ http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20573v1

## 5. Hindsight-Privileged RLVR: Single-Rollout Token Credit Without Group Sampling
*Relevance: 233%*

This paper addresses the problem of credit assignment in reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards, where group-sampling methods like GRPO fail when all attempts receive identical outcomes. HP-RLVR solves this by using a single rollout's own future trajectory to compute token-level update weights, eliminating the need for multiple samples per prompt while maintaining effective learning signals—making training more sample-efficient and practical for ML practitioners working with language model reasoning tasks.

→ http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20497v1
